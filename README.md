# First access review: a small-team workbook

Run one documented review of access to one system. This free, editable kit helps a small team record the population reviewed, decisions, changes and sign-off. It contains a [blank workbook](workbook.md), a [fictional completed example](worked-example.md), and an [evidence checklist](evidence-checklist.md).

**No account, email or purchase required.** Copy the Markdown files into your own document tool. All kit text is licensed under [CC BY 4.0](LICENSE.md); you may adapt and redistribute it with attribution. Never put real account exports or completed reviews in a public repository.

## Run the review

1. Choose one system and the review period. Name a system owner and reviewer with enough context to judge access. Use a second approver for the reviewer's own access where feasible; record any small-team separation limitations.
2. Export all in-scope accounts and their access, including disabled accounts, guests, service accounts and privileged roles. Record filters, export time, source and account count. If a source omits a category, obtain it separately and reconcile it. Store the raw export privately.
3. Compare accounts with your current employee/contractor roster, approved access requests and role requirements. Account for every exported account in the workbook. Identify duplicates, unexpected admins, leavers and ownerless service accounts.
4. Record a reasoned decision for each account/role: retain, change, remove or investigate. Unresolved questions stay open with an owner and deadline. A pending decision is not approval.
5. Implement changes through your normal authorized process. Record evidence, execution time and who verified the outcome. Do not delete a service account until its dependencies are assessed.
6. Reconcile totals, record unresolved exceptions, and obtain sign-off. Schedule the next review under your organization's policy and risk assessment; this kit does not prescribe a universal frequency.

## What this kit does and does not establish

This is an operational example for organizing logical-access review records, potentially useful when evidencing SOC 2 access controls. It is not a certification, legal advice, an auditor-approved format, or a guarantee that any control is designed or operating effectively. Define scope, review frequency, retention and acceptance criteria with your organization and auditor as appropriate.

Primary background: [AICPA Trust Services Criteria](https://www.aicpa-cima.com/resources/download/2017-trust-services-criteria-with-revised-points-of-focus-2022) and [NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/53/r5/upd1/final), particularly AC-2 (account management), AC-5 (separation of duties), and AC-6 (least privilege). These references are background, not a claim of complete framework coverage.
